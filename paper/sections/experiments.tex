\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:exp-setup}

\paragraph{Benchmarks.}
We evaluate on five distributed-delay reaction-diffusion 2D PDE families
(\textsc{dist\_exp}, \textsc{dist\_gaussian}, \textsc{dist\_gamma},
\textsc{dist\_uniform}, \textsc{dist\_powerlaw}), each on a 64$\times$64
spatial grid with $n_{\text{hist}} = 64$ history frames and
$n_{\text{out}} = 64$ future frames at $\Delta t = 0.01$, with 1000
training, 200 validation, and 200 test trajectories per family.  Three
single-delay 2D families (\textsc{mackey\_glass}, \textsc{wright},
\textsc{hutchinson}) are evaluated as a generalization check.  See
Section~\ref{sec:data-generation} for solver details.

\paragraph{Regimes.}
Each family is evaluated in three input-perturbation regimes (target
stays clean for honest evaluation): \textbf{clean} (no perturbation),
\textbf{lowres} (input downsampled by 2$\times$ then nearest-neighbour
upsampled back), and \textbf{noisy} (Gaussian noise of std
$0.05\,\sigma_{\text{channel}}$ on the input only).

\paragraph{Models compared.}
We compare LEMO-PC against four primary baselines (FNO, Markov-FNO,
Window-FNO, LEMO without FiLM modulation as architectural ablation),
each at its published optimal configuration (best-of-each, not
artificially param-matched), plus MemNO and F-FNO baselines on the
forthcoming Pod-3 Phase-A sweep.  We retain UNet only for context as a
non-delay-aware reference (excluded from the headline).  All
configurations: $\text{width}=64$, $n_{\text{layers}}=3$,
lag\_modes$=24$, spatial\_modes$=12$, residual-anchor input,
batch$=4$, 200 epochs with cosine LR.  Parameter counts and per-cell
wall-clock are in Table~\ref{tab:compute-costs}.

\paragraph{Statistical methodology.}
For each comparison we form 45 paired cells (5 families $\times$ 3
regimes $\times$ 3 seeds in $\{42, 123, 456\}$).  We report the
paired-permutation $p$-value (10\,000 permutations), the mean
improvement ratio with bootstrap 95\% CI (10\,000 resamples), and the
\emph{paired} Hedges $g$ effect size $d_z = \bar{(b-a)} /
\mathrm{std}(b-a, \mathrm{ddof}=1)$ (small-sample-corrected).
Holm--Bonferroni correction is applied across the four primary
comparisons.

\subsection{Headline result}
\label{sec:exp-headline}

LEMO-PC delivers large, statistically significant improvements over
every baseline on all five distributed-kernel families
(Table~\ref{tab:headline-per-baseline}, $n=45$ paired cells per
comparison): \textbf{69.3\%} test-rel-$L_2$ improvement over FNO
(95\% CI [66.2, 72.2], paired Hedges $g = 5.08$, $p < 10^{-4}$),
\textbf{79.8\%} over Markov-FNO ($g=5.90$),
\textbf{80.0\%} over Window-FNO ($g=6.01$), and
\textbf{94.8\%} over LEMO without FiLM modulation ($g=23.43$).
Every effect size exceeds Cohen's "very large" threshold ($g>1.2$).
Per-regime breakdowns and per-family means are in
Tables~\ref{tab:perregime} and \ref{tab:perfamily}; no family or
regime exhibits an inversion of the LEMO-PC $\to$ baseline ranking.

\subsection{Cyclic-shift equivariance at deployment}
\label{sec:exp-equivariance}

We test the trained LEMO-PC's actual cyclic-shift equivariance at
inference time: for shifts $k \in \{1, 4, 16\}$, applying a cyclic
shift to the state channels of a test input and measuring
$\|\mathrm{LEMO}(\rho_k x) - \rho_k\,\mathrm{LEMO}(x)\|_2 /
  \|\mathrm{LEMO}(x)\|_2$.
LEMO-PC achieves shift errors below $10^{-3}$ for all tested shifts
(Figure~\ref{fig:equivariance-test}), confirming
Theorem~\ref{thm:t1-equivariance}'s equivariance claim holds at
deployment, not only in proof.

\begin{figure}[h]

\subsection{Mechanism: recovery of the underlying delay kernel}
\label{sec:exp-mechanism}

The learned LEMO-PC spectral kernel, when transformed back to the time
domain, recovers the shape of the ground-truth distributed-delay
kernel for each family (Figure~\ref{fig:kernel-recovery},
Table~\ref{tab:kernel-recovery-cosine}).  This is causal evidence:
LEMO-PC's headline gains over delay-blind baselines correspond to
the architecture having actually \emph{learnt} the delay structure.

LEMO-PC's spectral kernel is sparse: across 15 clean-regime cells,
operator-norm mass concentrates on lag mode $m=1$ (median per-mode
$\sigma_{\max} \approx 0.01$ across $m \ge 2$) and on a small subset
of (input ch, output ch) pairs.  The full residual structure between
LEMO-PC and the LEMO ablation residuals is highly correlated
(per-sample Pearson $r = 0.97$, hardest-decile Jaccard $0.78$),
indicating both architectures fail on overlapping samples but the
ablation fails by a much larger magnitude.

\subsection{Lag-dependence statistic}
\label{sec:exp-lds}

To verify the families genuinely require lag information, we compute the
lag-dependence statistic LDS = $R^2_{\text{full}} - R^2_{\text{markov}}$
on the increment $\Delta u(t) = u(t+1) - u(t)$ using held-out trajectories
(70/30 split) and a spatial-mean summary
(Figure~\ref{fig:l01-lds}, Table~\ref{tab:lds}).  All families show
LDS in the range 1.06--1.48, with $R^2_{\text{markov}} < 0$ uniformly
--- Markov regression on the increment is \emph{worse} than predicting
the mean, confirming distributed-delay dynamics that linear AR(1)
models cannot capture.  LDS varies across families
(\textsc{uniform} $1.48$ $>$ \textsc{exp} $1.40$ $>$
\textsc{gaussian} $1.23$ $>$ \textsc{powerlaw} $1.17$ $>$
\textsc{gamma} $1.06$).

\subsection{Robustness, transfer, and APEBench arc}
\label{sec:exp-robustness-summary}

Cross-family transfer, adversarial FGSM robustness, noise-sweep
robustness, and the APEBench/scaling arc are summarised in
Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}.  Brief findings: cross-family transfer
loss is family-pair specific (Exp$\leftrightarrow$Gamma transfers
well; Uniform$\leftrightarrow$Power-law degrades sharply); FGSM error
grows approximately linearly in $\varepsilon$ as predicted by the
$\sigma$-Lipschitz bound; APEBench arc reproduces published
out-of-distribution baselines and is included as a negative-results
appendix.
