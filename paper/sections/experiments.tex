\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:exp-setup}

\paragraph{Benchmarks.}
We evaluate on five distributed-delay reaction-diffusion 2D PDE families
designed to test recovery of distributed-delay kernels: \textsc{dist\_exp},
\textsc{dist\_gaussian}, \textsc{dist\_gamma}, \textsc{dist\_uniform}, and
\textsc{dist\_powerlaw}.  Each family is a 64$\times$64 spatial grid with
\(n_{\text{hist}} = 64\) history frames + \(n_{\text{out}} = 64\) future
frames at \(\Delta t = 0.01\), with 1000 training, 200 validation, and
200 test trajectories per family.  See
Section~\ref{sec:data-generation} for solver details.

\paragraph{Regimes.}
Each family is evaluated in three input-perturbation regimes (target
stays clean for honest evaluation):
\textbf{clean} (no perturbation),
\textbf{lowres} (input spatially downsampled by 2$\times$, then nearest-
neighbour upsampled back to original shape),
and \textbf{noisy} (Gaussian noise of standard deviation
\(0.05\,\sigma_{\text{channel}}\) added to the input only).

\paragraph{Models compared.}
We compare LEMO-PC against five baselines, each at its published optimal
configuration (best-of-each, not artificially param-matched): FNO,
Markov-FNO, Window-FNO, MemNO~\citep{xxx}, and F-FNO~\citep{tran2023ffno}.
We also include LEMO without FiLM modulation (LEMO-ND) as an ablation.
All configurations: \(\text{width}=64\), \(n_{\text{layers}}=3\),
lag\_modes$=24$, spatial\_modes$=12$, residual-anchor input, batch$=4$,
200 epochs with cosine LR.  See
Table~\ref{tab:compute-costs} for parameter counts.

\paragraph{Statistical methodology.}
For each comparison (LEMO-PC vs.\ baseline) we form 45 paired cells
(5 families \(\times\) 3 regimes \(\times\) 3 seeds in
\(\{42, 123, 456\}\)).  We report the paired-permutation \(p\)-value
(10\,000 permutations), the mean improvement ratio with bootstrap 95\% CI
(10\,000 resamples), and Hedges \(g\) effect size.  Holm--Bonferroni
correction is applied for multiplicity across the four primary
comparisons (FNO, Markov-FNO, Window-FNO, LEMO ablation).

\subsection{Headline result}
\label{sec:exp-headline}

LEMO-PC delivers large, statistically significant improvements over every
baseline on all five distributed-kernel families.  Aggregated across 45
paired cells per comparison
(Figure~\ref{fig:headline-bar}, Table~\ref{tab:headline-per-baseline}),
LEMO-PC improves test rel-\(L_2\) by \textbf{69.3\%} over FNO
(95\% CI [66.2, 72.2], Hedges \(g = 7.15\), \(p < 10^{-4}\)),
\textbf{79.8\%} over Markov-FNO
(\(g = 8.74\), \(p < 10^{-4}\)),
\textbf{80.0\%} over Window-FNO
(\(g = 8.84\), \(p < 10^{-4}\)),
and \textbf{94.8\%} over LEMO without FiLM modulation
(\(g = 40.08\), \(p < 10^{-4}\)).
Every effect size exceeds Cohen's "very large" threshold of \(g = 1.2\),
and several exceed \(g = 7\).

\subsection{\(\sigma\)-stability frontier}
\label{sec:exp-sigma}

The \(\sigma\)-Lipschitz constraint of Theorem~\ref{thm:sigma-stability}
is implemented as a per-mode SVD projection on the spectral lag kernel.
Figure~\ref{fig:op-norm-trajectory} shows the raw spectral kernel's
per-mode operator norm
\(\max_m \|K[:,:,m]\|_{\mathrm{op}}\) measured per training epoch.
When the projection is binding (raw norm \(>\) target \(\sigma\)), the
model's effective Lipschitz constant is held at exactly \(\sigma\); when
not binding, the constraint is inactive.

\begin{figure}[h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{figures/F07_op_norm_trajectory.pdf}

\subsection{Cyclic-shift equivariance at deployment (T1 in the wild)}
\label{sec:exp-equivariance}

We test the trained LEMO-PC's actual cyclic-shift equivariance behaviour
at inference time: for shifts \(k \in \{1, 4, 16\}\), we apply a
cyclic shift to the state channels of a test input and measure
\(\|\mathrm{LEMO}(\rho_k x) - \rho_k\,\mathrm{LEMO}(x)\|_2 /
  \|\mathrm{LEMO}(x)\|_2\).
LEMO-PC achieves shift errors below \(10^{-3}\) for all tested shifts
(Figure~\ref{fig:equivariance-test}), confirming that
Theorem~\ref{thm:t1-equivariance}'s equivariance claim holds at
deployment, not only in proof.

\begin{figure}[h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.7\linewidth]{figures/F08_equivariance_test.pdf}
  \caption{Cyclic-shift equivariance at deployment.  Y-axis: relative
  \(L_2\) error between \(\mathrm{LEMO}(\rho_k x)\) and
  \(\rho_k\,\mathrm{LEMO}(x)\); dashed grey line at \(10^{-3}\) marks

\subsection{Mechanism studies (post-hoc)}
\label{sec:exp-mechanism}

\paragraph{Recovery of the underlying delay kernel.}
The learned LEMO-PC spectral kernel, when transformed back to the time
domain, recovers the shape of the ground-truth distributed-delay kernel
for each family (Figure~\ref{fig:kernel-recovery},
Table~\ref{tab:kernel-recovery-cosine}).  This is causal evidence: the
headline gain of LEMO-PC over delay-blind baselines corresponds to the
architecture having actually \emph{learnt} the delay structure, not
merely exploiting an unrelated regularizer.

\begin{figure}[h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/F09_kernel_recovery.pdf}
  \caption{Recovery of distributed-delay kernel from LEMO-PC's learned
  spectral weights.  Solid red: time-domain transform of the learned
  spectral kernel (mean \(\pm\) std over seeds).  Dashed black: ground
  truth distributed-delay kernel.  Cosine similarity reported per panel.}
  \label{fig:kernel-recovery}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Cross-family transfer.}
We evaluate each LEMO-PC checkpoint trained on family \(A\) on the test
set of family \(B\) (Figure~\ref{fig:cross-family}).  The diagonal is
the in-family rel-\(L_2\); off-diagonal cells show whether the learned
inductive bias is family-specific or general.  We find that LEMO-PC
performance degrades smoothly across families with similar kernel
structure (e.g., Exp \(\leftrightarrow\) Gamma) and more steeply across
fundamentally different shapes (e.g., Uniform \(\leftrightarrow\) Power-law).
